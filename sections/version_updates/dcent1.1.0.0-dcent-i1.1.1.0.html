DCENT v1.1.0.0 · DCENT-I v1.1.1.0

[2026/08/10] Release Update

For ICOADS SST measurements from 1982 onward, DCENT v1.1.0.0 applies an additional quality-control check that makes use of information from the satellite era.

Specifically, same-day OISST v2.1 fields are used as a common reference for identifying in-situ measurements with exceptionally large discrepancies. Although OISST v2.1 is not a purely satellite-based product, it is available with rapid daily updates, making it more suitable for this purpose than products such as ESA CCI, which are updated less frequently. The purpose of this comparison is not to treat OISST v2.1 as ground truth, but to exclude extreme discrepancies that are likely to reflect poor-quality in-situ measurements and thereby improve the consistency of measurements going into DCENT(-I).

Methods

Outlier SST measurements are identified using the median absolute deviation (MAD), rather than the mean and standard deviation, because the measurement differences are strongly non-Gaussian and may contain extreme values.

MAD = median(|xi|)

where xi is the difference between in-situ and satellite measurements. The robust scale estimate is:

σ̂MAD = 1.4826 × MAD

The scaling factor is derived from the standard normal distribution:

1.4826 = 1 / Φ−1(0.75)

The median absolute deviation was preferred over higher quantiles because it is less sensitive to contamination by outliers. This procedure assumes that the differences between the in-situ and satellite measurements are centered on zero.

The robust scale estimates were calculated separately for ship and buoy measurements for each calendar month and each 5° grid cell. For each grid cell–month combination, all collocated in-situ and satellite differences from 1982–2025 were pooled to estimate a single value of σ̂MAD. The resulting scale estimates are typically around 1.0–1.5°C for ship measurements and 0.2–0.4°C for buoy measurements.

Values are flagged as potential outliers when:

|xi| > kσ̂MAD

where k = 2.58 for ship measurements and k = 3.29 for buoy measurements. These values correspond to nominal two-sided Gaussian equivalent coverage levels of 99% and 99.9%, respectively. The larger multiplier for buoy measurements provides a more conservative operational threshold, reducing the risk of rejecting otherwise reliable buoy observations.

An additional platform-level check was applied after the individual observation check. Within each processing month, observations sharing the same platform identifiers were grouped together, and the median in-situ–satellite difference was calculated using only observations that passed the preceding individual-level check. The median, Mp, was then used to reduce the influence of any remaining extreme differences.

|Mp| > kσp

All observations associated with a platform were rejected when this condition was met, where σp = 0.71°C and k = 2.58 for ships, and σp = 0.29°C and k = 3.29 for buoys. The σp values were adopted from Kennedy et al. (2012). This additional step is intended to identify platforms exhibiting a persistent systematic discrepancy from the satellite reference, even when some individual observations are not sufficiently extreme to be rejected separately.

Results

The effect of this quality-control procedure is illustrated below using ICOADS SST measurements from June 2026, shown before and after the additional OISST-based checks. The additional quality control removes clearly anomalous ship-track features, including excessively warm measurements in the southern Indian Ocean and excessively cold measurements in the Pacific warm pool. After gridding, this reduces isolated warm and cold artifacts and produces a spatially more coherent SST anomaly field.

Overall, the additional OISST-based quality control is relatively selective for ship and buoy observations. Across 1982–2025, approximately 93% of ship and 96% of buoy measurements that passed the preceding quality-control procedures are retained after the additional checks. The corresponding retention rate is substantially lower for CMAN observations, only at approximately 60%. This larger reduction primarily reflects the platform-level check, which removes all observations from CMAN stations exhibiting persistent systematic discrepancies from the OISST reference.

The relatively large fraction of rejected CMAN measurements are not expected totranslate into a comparably large effect on the gridded open-ocean SST fields. CMAN observations are collected at fixed, high-frequency stations concentrated along the U.S. coast, so individual stations can contribute large numbers of measurements while representing only a limited spatial area.

Implications for DCENT and DCENT-I

The implications of the additional QC step were assessed by comparing global SST estimates with and without its application. For DCENT SST, the 1982–2025 warming trend increases from 0.145 to 0.158°C per decade, a difference of 0.012°C per decade.

This difference reflects both the removal of genuinely anomalous measurements and any potential influence associated with the choice of OISST v2.1 as the reference. The latter is of particular interest because Menemenlis et al. (2025) reported that OISST v2.1's 1982–2024 warming trend is approximately 0.03–0.05°C per decade larger than those of COBE2 and ERSST v5. Nevertheless, the 0.012°C per decade change associated with the additional QC is substantially smaller than these inter-product trend differences.

Infilling further reduces the difference between estimates with and without the additional QC. For DCENT-I SST, the trend difference decreases to 0.005°C per decade. The reduction from 0.012 to 0.005°C per decade is consistent with a substantial part of the original difference arising from localized anomalous grid cells that are suppressed by the infilling procedure. The remaining difference provides a conservative measure of the residual sensitivity to the QC, including any potential dependence on the OISST v2.1 reference.

The corresponding impact on global mean surface temperature is smaller still. The 1982–2025 DCENT-I GMST trend increases from 0.217 to 0.221°C per decade when the additional QC is applied, a difference of 0.004°C decade⁻¹, or about 1.9% of the estimated warming rate. This change is also small relative to the ensemble uncertainty in the DCENT-I trend (95% CI: 0.210–0.225°C per decade).

Thus, although some dependence on the OISST v2.1 reference cannot be excluded, its potential influence on the final DCENT-I estimate of global warming is limited.
